# Fix the data license, then sell the automation

The module is a credible seed for a sellable platform, but its value is not the TecDoc lookup. The lookup depends on an unverified RapidAPI wrapper, and persisting its payloads (`raw_data`) probably violates the terms that govern genuine TecDoc data: TecAlliance's own general terms cap caching at **30 days**, forbid sublicensing, and require deletion of all copies on breach ([TecAlliance GTC](https://a.storyblok.com/f/297549/x/d6dca87535/gtc-en.pdf)). The defensible, sellable assets are the parts the owner controls: the OEM matching and normalization engine, supplier price-list ingestion, the review workflow, and the Odoo sync. Those should be rebuilt as a thin Odoo connector plus an external matching and orchestration service, with catalog data hidden behind swappable provider adapters. Engineering priorities, in order: stop treating raw TecDoc payloads as permanent storage, move upstream calls and matching out of Odoo's HTTP workers, replace the blanket `base.group_user` CRUD rights with roles, and upgrade the fuzzy script into a staged matcher with human review. Commercially, a three-tier offer (roughly $99-199, $350-800 and $1,500-4,000 per month, all my estimates) sits between $45-349 Odoo add-ons and enterprise pricing tools. Most prices, ROI figures and market sizes in the evidence are vendor-sourced or estimated, so the plan should be validated through three to five paid Egyptian pilots before any Gulf expansion.

## The RapidAPI data source is the weakest link and the biggest legal exposure

Official TecDoc is a negotiated commercial license with no public price. Cost depends on data volume, number of end users and distribution model, and is renegotiated annually ([Dev-opla guide](https://dev-opla.com/resources/what-is-tecdoc-auto-parts-ecommerce-guide)). TecAlliance's general terms (v3.2, Feb 2023) grant a "non-exclusive, non-transferable, and non-sublicensable" license tied to the named project (cl. 1.6.2). They let a customer use a service provider such as an Odoo integrator only after notifying TecAlliance and having the provider sign an NDA (cl. 1.6.5). They allow temporary caching only until the next data update and never beyond 30 days, and forbid caching usage-billed data entirely (cl. 1.9). On uncured breach, rights lapse, all copies and backups must be deleted, and a contractual penalty applies (cl. 1.11) ([TecAlliance GTC](https://a.storyblok.com/f/297549/x/d6dca87535/gtc-en.pdf)). I could not retrieve the RapidAPI listing, its plans, or any evidence that the wrapper is a TecAlliance-licensed reseller. An unofficial TecDoc extraction market is visible (Apify actors, freelance pipelines) ([Apify](https://apify.com/making-data-meaningful/tecdoc), [Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/mhamzaanwar1/build-tecdoc-automotive-parts-data-pipelines-with-compatibility-sets)). My inference, not a verified fact, is that a low-cost marketplace wrapper is more likely unlicensed than a hidden official resale arrangement. Reselling that content to customers inside a product would compound the problem. RapidAPI's own acceptable-use policy also prohibits resale of API access without authorization, and its terms leave each API's terms between consumer and provider ([RapidAPI Terms](https://rapidapi.com/terms)). None of this is legal advice; counsel should review before any data reaches a paying customer.

The practical mitigation has four steps. First, ask the wrapper vendor in writing for proof of license and permitted caching or redistribution, and request a regional quote from TecAlliance Middle East, which has a Dubai entity ([Automechanika Dubai](https://exhibitor.automechanikadubai.com/exhibitor-search-en/exhibitor-search.detail.html/tecalliance-middle-east.html)). Second, treat TecDoc-derived data as lookup-only: keep your own keys (article number plus supplier ID) and a provenance record rather than payload copies, and put raw responses in an expiring cache with a purge job. Third, tag every stored record with `provider`, `license_class`, `fetched_at` and `expires_at`, so a cache TTL of at most 30 days applies to TecDoc-class data while owned data (supplier price lists, your own cross-references) is kept indefinitely. Fourth, build first-party data as the long-term moat, because the cross-references a distributor confirms through human review belong to it and carry no third-party license.

Coverage is a second reason to diversify. The UAE passenger fleet is about **66% Japanese** and 7.55% Korean, and GCC-wide the split is about 61% Japanese and 14% Korean ([TradeArabia](https://www.tradearabia.com/news/MTR_159038.html), snippet-level). TecDoc's strength is European vehicles, though TecAlliance has added Asia-Pacific K-Type data and Korean and Japanese aftermarket brands with 330,000+ fitment links ([PARts DB](https://partsdb.com.au/training-education/parts-db/new-korean-and-japanese-brands-join-tecalliance/)). I found no source quantifying coverage of GCC-spec trims or Chinese brands (Chery, Geely, MG, BYD) in any provider, so coverage must be tested per brand in a pilot. For VIN decoding, NHTSA vPIC is free but excludes vehicles not sold in the US and is asked to be batched off-peak ([NHTSA FAQ](https://vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov/api/home/index/faq)). Commercial decoders such as Vincario run **EUR 0.22-0.49 per lookup** by volume ([Vincario](https://vincario.com/blog/?p=698), vendor source). OEM catalog vendors such as Laximo cover 45 brands, but their pricing and API terms are not public ([Soware.ru](https://soware.ru/products/laximo-cat)). Partslink24 is not an option for automation, since its terms bar robots, scripts and AI agents and forbid saving content to other media ([partslink24 ToS](https://legal.lexcom.de/legaldocs/partslink24-tos/-/10/en/tos-prev.html)). ACES/PIES from the Auto Care Association are worth adopting as the schema for fitment and product data (entry package **$2,500 per year**, JSON/SQL delivery), but they cover North and Latin America and contain no MENA vehicle data ([Auto Care](https://www.autocare.org/data-and-information/data-standards/subscriptions)).

## An Odoo connector plus an external service beats a bigger module

The current module makes blocking RapidAPI calls from Odoo with `time.sleep(0.5)` and a 15-second timeout, stores the key in `ir.config_parameter`, and grants every internal user full CRUD. Each of those is a known weakness, and each has a standard fix. Odoo's HTTP workers are scarce and bounded by wall-clock limits, so long batch work belongs in `queue_job`, whose channels cap per-channel concurrency and which supports escalating retry patterns such as `{1: 60, 2: 180, 3: 10, 5: 300}` ([queue_job](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/13.0/queue_job)). A `time.sleep` holds a worker and a database transaction. A shared token bucket per upstream key, kept in Redis in the external service, is the proper quota mechanism, and queue_job channel capacity can complement it inside Odoo. The `ir.config_parameter` store is plain text, visible to system administrators and included in database backups ([Braincuber](https://www.braincuber.com/tutorial/securing-api-keys-sensitive-data-odoo-best-practices), a lower-reliability vendor blog). Better options are environment variables or a vault at service level, and community modules such as `generic_crypto_utils` for encrypted parameters ([Odoo Apps](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/18.0/generic_crypto_utils)). I could not fetch Odoo's own security documentation, so group and record-rule specifics here are engineering judgment: separate viewer, reviewer and admin groups, record rules per company or tenant, signature checks on any `auth='none'` endpoint, and a remedy for the raw JSON field in the form of a normalized table plus a separate immutable `raw_payload` table with hash and provenance.

The recommended architecture splits the work by what Odoo is good at. A thin Odoo module keeps models, security, review views and `queue_job` tasks that push and pull orders, prices and stock. An external FastAPI service with its own Postgres (pgvector, pg_trgm) and Redis owns provider adapters, quota management, caching, normalization and matching. This split is also the only portable one, because Odoo.sh cannot install PostgreSQL extensions ([Odoo.sh FAQ](https://www.odoo.sh/faq)), Odoo Online restricts the external API to Custom plans, and the stock queue_job runner has acceptable-use implications on Odoo.sh ([VentorTech](https://ecosystem.ventor.tech/faq/e-commerce-connectors/common-questions/why-our-connectors-use-job-queue-queue_job-and-why-its-safe-to-use-on-odoo-sh/)). For the Odoo API, Odoo 19 introduces a bearer-token JSON-2 endpoint (`/json/2/<model>/<method>`), and XML-RPC and JSON-RPC are scheduled for removal in Odoo 22 in fall 2028 per the Odoo docs ([Odoo 19 docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/developer/reference/external_api.html)). That conflicts with a separate report that places removal in Odoo 20 ([n8n issue #21545](https://github.com/n8n-io/n8n/issues/21545)), so the target version must be checked. A small adapter class (XML-RPC for 17/18, JSON-2 for 19+) makes migration a configuration switch. The n8n Odoo node was reported still using the deprecated endpoints against Odoo 19, so n8n should call JSON-2 through its HTTP Request node.

Resilience and observability follow from the same service. Every pull or write job needs an idempotency key (the IETF Idempotency-Key header draft is a usable convention ([IETF](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header))). Retries need exponential backoff with jitter that honors `Retry-After`, and each upstream needs a circuit breaker. Webhooks should land in an inbox table after signature verification and be processed asynchronously, while Odoo-to-service events use an outbox. An append-only audit table should record actor, action and correlation ID. For observability, the documented FastAPI pattern exports OpenTelemetry traces to Tempo, metrics to Prometheus and trace-linked logs to Loki, viewed in Grafana ([blueswen/fastapi-observability](https://github.com/blueswen/fastapi-observability)). Passing the queue_job UUID as a `traceparent` joins Odoo jobs to service traces. The evidence for these resilience, testing and CI/CD practices is mostly my own judgment rather than sourced. That includes a golden-set regression test for matching precision and recall in CI, recorded upstream fixtures, and consumer-driven contract tests between the Odoo adapter and the service. Treat them as a proposed standard rather than documented best practice.

For packaging, Odoo multi-tenancy means one database per tenant, not multi-company inside one database ([OEC](https://oec.sh/blog/odoo-multi-tenant-architecture), vendor blog). The suggested model is single-tenant Odoo per client, a thin OPL-1 connector module, and a shared multi-tenant service keyed by tenant with row-level security and per-tenant quotas. OPL-1 licensing is what the Odoo Apps Store accepts for paid apps, and it forbids redistribution and sublicensing of copies ([OPL-1 text](https://open.windriver.com/info/uni-license-list/licenses/odoo-pl-1.0.html)). Because customers can read module source, tier enforcement (plan, feature flags, quotas) must live server-side, delivered as a signed entitlement token. Code copied from LGPL or AGPL OCA modules should be kept separate from proprietary code, and a lawyer should confirm the licensing conclusions, which here are secondary-sourced.

## Staged matching and n8n workflows remove the data entry that costs the most

The RapidFuzz script (`token_sort_ratio`, threshold 75) has a structural problem: it is order-insensitive and treats identifiers as prose. Standard fuzzy matching misses or wrongly matches alphanumeric codes, which need exact comparison on normalized characters ([Alteryx community](https://community.alteryx.com/discussion/1344093/matching-fuzzy-match-or-other-techniques), [US7464101](https://patents.justia.com/patent/7464101)). The upgrade path is staged, and it is my engineering design rather than a sourced benchmark. Stage 0 normalizes part numbers (uppercase, strip punctuation and spaces, resolve O/0 and I/1 confusables) and resolves exact matches through an alias and supersession table. Stage 1 fuses pg_trgm and multilingual embeddings by reciprocal rank fusion, which is the documented hybrid pattern ([Crunchy Data](https://www.crunchydata.com/blog/hybrid-vector-search)), filtered by brand and category. BGE-M3 handles Arabic and English in dense, sparse and multi-vector modes ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/sayed0am/arabic-english-bge-m3)), and since pgvector 0.8.0, iterative index scans fix filtered queries returning too few rows ([pgedge docs](https://docs.pgedge.com/pgvector/v0-8-5/iterative-index-scans/)). Stage 2 reranks the top 5-10 candidates with a cross-encoder or LLM that returns structured JSON (match, confidence, reason), with part-number tokens as hard constraints. Arabic text needs normalization of alef variants, diacritics, tatweel and Arabic-Indic digits, plus a synonym dictionary for common parts. Postgres has no robust built-in Arabic full-text configuration, so trigram plus normalized text plus embeddings is the practical route ([PostgreSQL lists](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/Pine.LNX.4.64.0902021746080.4158%40sn.sai.msu.ru)). No public Arabic auto-parts benchmark exists, so the threshold of 75 is an unvalidated default until tuned on a few hundred labeled pairs from the client's own data.

Human review is the product's trust mechanism, and its data model should be explicit. Each candidate stores per-signal scores, fused score and model version. Decisions are append-only (auto-accepted, needs review, accepted, rejected, superseded), with confidence above a high threshold auto-accepted, a middle band routed to a review queue sorted by value at risk, and the low band rejected or suggested as a new part. Reviewer corrections feed the alias table and the evaluation set. Price lists are versioned rather than updated in place, and activating a version is a transaction that closes the previous one and syncs to Odoo `product.supplierinfo` through idempotent jobs with a dry-run diff. This design is my recommendation, and I found no sourced reference architecture for auto-parts review interfaces.

n8n's best role is as the orchestration layer around that service. The strongest use case is supplier price-list ingestion: an email trigger, a file-type router, deterministic parsing for Excel and CSV, OCR plus an LLM with a JSON-schema output parser for PDFs and scans, validation (price above zero, currency allow-list, part-number pattern, price delta against the previous list), and a review queue. Published n8n templates follow this pattern ([GrowwStacks](https://www.growwstacks.com/workflows/invoice-data-extraction-with-llamaparse-and-openai)), but the 95-98% accuracy claims come from vendor blogs about invoices rather than price lists ([Cradl AI](https://cradl.ai/posts/how-to-extract-data-from-invoices-in-n8n)). The cheaper and more accurate lever is using the LLM for header and column mapping per supplier rather than for row-by-row extraction (my judgment). The second use case is WhatsApp request-for-quote handling. A vendor-authored LatAm case study (12,000 SKUs) reports quote time down from 14 to 4 minutes (**-71%**), 67% of quotes fully automated, misorders cut from 6.3% to 3.9%, and a 5.2-month payback, with the authors concluding that "ROI lives in misorders, not time savings" ([EITS](https://eitserv.tech/en/blog/caso-distribuidor-repuestos-ia)). That points the product's headline metric at wrong-match rate and returns, not hours saved. WhatsApp matters here, with about 52 million users in Egypt and 29 million in Saudi Arabia ([Chatdaddy Egypt](https://chatdaddy.tech/blog/whatsapp-business-api-egypt), [Chatdaddy Saudi](https://chatdaddy.tech/blog/whatsapp-business-api-saudi-arabia), vendor blogs). Meta bills per delivered template message since July 2025, with inbound-initiated service windows free ([Meta docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/)). Secondary sources claim service messages become billable from 1 October 2026, which the fetched Meta page did not confirm, so check the current rate card before budgeting ([Wati](https://www.wati.io/en/blog/whatsapp-service-message-pricing/)). A VIN must be resolved through licensed catalog data, and an LLM should never map a VIN to part numbers on its own.

Customs paperwork is a third, narrower use case. Egypt's Advance Cargo Information regime requires a 19-digit ACID on the invoice, packing list and bill of lading, with documents submitted at least 48 hours before departure ([getctn](https://getctn.com/en/guides/egypt-acid-comprehensive-guide), [APL Logistics](https://www.apllogistics.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Customer-Advisory-Egypt-Advance-Cargo-Information-ACI.pdf)). OCR plus an LLM can pre-fill fields from supplier documents, but I found no public Nafeza API, so a broker still files. LLM HS-code classification is weak at fine granularity: one benchmark has a fine-tuned 70B model at **57.5% at six digits and 40% at ten** ([ATLAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18400), snippet-level), and vendor claims of 96% are unverified ([GingerControl](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/automated-hs-code-accuracy-96-percent)). The safe design keeps a verified HS table keyed to product family, proposes codes only for new items constrained to the valid tariff list, and stores the broker-approved answer. Competitor price monitoring should be limited to public pages and supplier feeds under agreement: US case law on scraping public data is permissive but contract claims survive ([hiQ v. LinkedIn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HiQ_Labs_v._LinkedIn)), and under EU law, a site owner may restrict scraping contractually ([SCL on Ryanair](https://www.scl.org/3280-the-cjeu-takes-flight-databases-and-air-fares/)). Egyptian and GCC scraping law was not researched.

One constraint shapes how n8n is sold. The Sustainable Use License allows internal use and consulting that builds workflows for clients, but forbids charging for hosted access or white-labeling, and hosting several customers' workflows and credentials on one commercial instance needs an Enterprise license (secondary sources; the primary page returned 404) ([Nordflux](https://nordflux.de/en/guides/n8n-sustainable-use-license-erklaert)). Options are one n8n instance per client with the client as licensee, an Enterprise or Embed license from n8n, or an MIT-licensed alternative such as Activepieces ([Activepieces](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/automation-pricing.md)). Queue mode requires Postgres, Redis and separate workers ([n8n docs](https://docs.N8n.io/hosting/scaling/queue-mode/)). Heavy matching should stay in the Python service that n8n calls, not inside n8n workflows.

## Price between $45 add-ons and enterprise tools, and bill upstream costs through

The market has clear price anchors. Odoo marketplace auto-parts add-ons cost **$45** (Binary Bridge, with cross-references) and from **$349** (ECOSIRE, with fitment, OEM interchange and supersession chains) ([Odoo Apps](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/auto_parts), [ECOSIRE](https://ecosire.com/de/apps/odoo/automobile-spare-parts)). Price monitoring SaaS runs about $99 per month (100 SKUs) to $399 per month (5,000 SKUs) for Prisync ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/153451/Prisync/pricing/)) and from about $40 to over $1,000 per month for Price2Spy by URL volume ([PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/price2spy)). Competera is custom enterprise pricing ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/compare/136974-10025214/Competera-Pricing-Platform-vs-Price-Observatory)). Workshop ordering tools are cheap: PartsTech is free to about $95 per month ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/231766/PartsTech/pricing/)). The gap is an importer-side tool that combines OEM cross-referencing, supplier price ingestion and Odoo sync, which none of these anchors offers (my inference from the competitors I could price). I did not research Autoline, Webcat, Ankor, Epicor, Infor, Nexpart or local AI automation agencies, so the competitive map is incomplete and needs a dedicated pass before launch. A bare module cannot charge more than the add-on range, so the recurring value must come from the data and automation service.

The tiers below are my estimates, not sourced benchmarks. Starter is an Egypt-first importer with one user, up to about 2,000 SKUs, three supplier sources and manual-trigger matching and Excel import-export, at **$500-1,500 setup and $99-199 per month**. Growth covers up to 15,000 SKUs, ten suppliers, daily price monitoring, Arabic/English OEM matching, Odoo sync, WhatsApp quote export and five seats, at **$3,000-8,000 setup and $350-800 per month**. Enterprise or Gulf covers 50,000+ SKUs, multi-branch operation, API access, an SLA, custom ERP connectors and in-region hosting, at **$10,000-30,000 setup and $1,500-4,000 per month**, or $20,000-60,000 annually. Gulf pricing can sit at roughly 1.5-2.5 times Egypt pricing. Setup fees are consistent with published Odoo implementation bands (a marketplace connector at $3,000-8,000, annual support at 10-20% of build cost) ([ECOSIRE](https://ecosire.com/ja/blog/odoo-implementation-cost-breakdown-2026)). Egyptian development rates start around $20 per hour in one marketing mention, against $125-250 in North America (same source, weakly sourced for Egypt). Billing should be in USD, or AED and SAR for the Gulf, with an EGP-indexed clause for Egyptian SMEs, since the pound moved about 10% in a month and ranged 46.6-54.85 per USD in 2026 ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/egypt/currency)).

Unit economics depend on pass-through data costs. At 2026 list prices of $3/$15 per million tokens for Sonnet 4.6 and $1/$5 for Haiku 4.5 ([Finout](https://finout.io/blog/claude-pricing-in-2026-for-individuals-organizations-and-developers)), a 1,500-input, 300-output-token matching call costs about **$0.003 on Haiku and $0.009 on Sonnet** by my arithmetic, so 10,000 calls a month cost $30-90 before caching. An included call allowance per tier with overage at two to three times cost keeps AI gross margin high. The larger and less certain cost is catalog data. A UK reseller offered an annual TecDoc license at GBP 600 including the first 5,000 products, but that is 2019 and UK-only data ([ChannelX](https://channelx.world/2019/12/optiseller-give-cost-effective-tecdoc-catalogue-access-for-auto-parts-traders/)). The contract must therefore contain a pass-through clause for upstream license and price changes, a client-owns-its-data clause, a platform-IP-retained clause, and no uptime promise beyond upstream providers'. An illustrative Growth-tier margin of 55-70% at $550 revenue assumes $60-120 of data cost, which is unverified. As an ROI frame, a clerk costing an estimated EGP 8-15k per month (unsourced) means a 50% time saving on supplier updates pays back a $350 plan in under two months. This is an assumption to confirm in pilots, and the EITS case suggests that returns, not clerical time, drive real savings.

Personal-data rules shape the AI design. Product and price data are mostly not personal data, but customer contacts and staff accounts are. Egypt's PDPL (Law 151/2020) now has executive regulations (Ministerial Decision 816/2025) with a one-year compliance countdown and approval requirements for cross-border transfers ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/egypt-s-pdpl-executive-regulations-issued-one-year-compliance-countdown-begins), [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/egypt-s-personal-data-protection-law-the-compliance-countdown-has-begun)). Saudi Arabia and the UAE also restrict transfers abroad ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2024/02/saudi-arabias-new-personal-data-protection-law-in-force), [PwC](https://pwc.com/m1/en/publications/documents/2024/navigating-cross-border-data-transfers-key-regulations-in-the-middle-east.pdf)). Masking contacts before any LLM call, a data-processing agreement with a subprocessor list, and an in-region hosting option for the Enterprise tier address this.

## A phased roadmap that earns revenue before it scales

Market context supports starting in Egypt. Egypt's parts market is estimated at **$1-2 billion** and import-driven, with China, Korea and Japan as dominant suppliers ([MSC Egypt](https://www.msc.com/en/local-information/africa/egypt/automotive)). Counterfeit and parallel-market pressure is documented, though the sources are dated ([Masrawy](https://www.masrawy.com/autos/autos_news/details/2018/10/16/1445541/)). Saudi aftermarket estimates conflict from $4.8 billion to about $15 billion depending on definition ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/saudi-arabia-auto-parts-aftermarket), [Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/saudi-arabia-automotive-aftermarket-size-share-growth-drivers-trends-opportunities-forecast-20252030-market)), so the report uses ranges rather than a single number. I found no quantified survey of data-entry pain among MENA importers, so that pain is a hypothesis for pilots to test.

Phase one (months 0-3) removes legal and reliability risk and proves value. Confirm or replace the data source, add provenance and TTL fields, purge permanent raw payloads, and move upstream calls to `queue_job`. Replace blanket permissions with roles, add the staged matcher with a labeled evaluation set, build Excel/CSV price-list ingestion with review, and write the contract, DPA and licensing templates. Sign three to five paid pilots at $500-1,500 setup credited against subscription, and record a baseline of minutes per supplier sheet, auto-match rate, wrong-match rate and quote turnaround. The milestone is three paying pilots with measured hours and error rates.

Phase two (months 3-6) productizes. Add self-serve onboarding, usage metering with an LLM allowance, tiered billing, WhatsApp quote export, a competitor price-monitoring MVP limited to public and supplier-provided sources, and an Egypt PDPL review. Decide the n8n licensing route (per-client instances, a negotiated license or Activepieces) and the architecture split (hosted multi-tenant service versus dedicated deployment). The planning milestone is 10 paying accounts and $2-4k MRR, an estimate. Phase three (months 6-12) opens the Gulf with a local reseller partner, an in-region hosting option, an Odoo App Store listing under OPL-1, an API tier, a JSON-2 adapter and a negotiated TecDoc or OEM catalog license that explicitly permits what the product does. The planning milestone is 25-40 accounts, $10-15k MRR and a first Enterprise contract. The phase two and three milestone numbers are assumptions, not benchmarks.

## Conclusion

The central insight is that the original module's visible features, catalog lookup and fuzzy matching, are the least defensible parts of a platform, and its hidden choices (permanent raw payloads, one wrapper vendor, synchronous calls, open permissions) are what block a sale. The evidence suggests inverting the emphasis: treat third-party catalog data as a replaceable, license-bounded input, and make the owner-controlled layer the product, which is confirmed cross-references, normalized Arabic/English matching, versioned supplier price lists and an audited review workflow. That layer is also what lets pricing escape the $45-349 add-on range.

Three questions decide the plan and cannot be answered from this evidence: what the RapidAPI vendor's license actually permits, which Odoo versions the target customers run (which sets the JSON-2 timing), and how well any provider covers GCC-spec and Chinese-brand vehicles. Each can be settled quickly, the first by a written request to the vendor and a TecAlliance Middle East quote, and the last by a coverage test on a client's real parts list. Answering them before building Phase two avoids rebuilding the data layer after customers depend on it.
